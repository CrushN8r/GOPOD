## What's running

GOPOD already has a content engine turning — the wordplay, the niche pillars, the songs
feeding it, all real and built. The robots perform. The audience-facing side — the sites, the
socials, the schedule that keeps people coming back — is the part that needs a hand. Not
starting from nothing. Starting from something that already moves and needs someone to carry
it wide.

## The role

One pro-level person. Open to a small team if that's what it takes, but the expectation is
one person who can own this. Once GOPOD's set up, the ongoing work is largely cookie-cutter —
keep it running, keep growth balanced. This isn't a one-off gig. This person stays on.

**Scope:**
- Social media accounts — set up, running, on a schedule
- Social media + the niche-pillar sites — the content orbit around GOPOD's core
- Website administration — making the sites real, not "under construction"
- Content generation and scheduling — the machine that keeps it fed

**How you help — open, not forced.** Coaching (teach me to run it myself) or hands-on (run it
for me) — both are genuinely on the table. Which one matters less than this: it grows, and
keeps growing, once it's yours to hold.

## Why this role matters

MY_GOPOD_ASK.md is my resume to an engineer — here's the running thing, here's the ceiling I
hit. This is my resume to a pro on the business side: here's the running thing, here's the
role that scales it. This is the operational piece a WEtech or Small Business Centre advisor
would expect to see before calling GOPOD investable — not just a cool build, a running
operation. That's what this role turns it into.

## Where it stands today

Honest, no drama — this is where the role comes in, not a confession:

- The websites are up, but "under construction." Real content, not yet a finished front.
- crushn8r.ca's newsletter infrastructure exists; it isn't yet a real, running list.
- The social accounts are named, not yet on a schedule.
- The content engine (AI Wordplay) is real and producing; consistent posting isn't there yet.

## If this becomes a paid role

If and when this turns into a paid position, the shape would likely be a retainer or
project-based arrangement, with growth measured by real reach — not vanity numbers. That's a
conversation for later, not a job posting now. The ask today is simpler: does this look like
something worth being the ops hand for?
