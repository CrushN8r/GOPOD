## The example: `foodmath.crushn8r.net`

There's a printable chocolate bingo party kit sitting in a brobot show's own files right
now. Real, working recipes, made so a birthday party has something to bake, not written
to prove a point about maths. Turns out they already were.

### recipe-math-plan

**1. Brobots Fudge Bolt Squares — no-bake, makes 16**
3 cups chocolate chips, one can of condensed milk, 2 tbsp butter. Want 32 squares
instead of 16? You don't just double every line. The pan size, the chill time, and the
ratio between chocolate and condensed milk are what actually set the fudge — get that
ratio wrong and it never firms up, no matter how long it sits. *That's not a baking
tip. That's the maths the recipe was already testing you on.*

**2. Brobots Chocolate Cookies with Nuts — makes 24**
Brown sugar *and* white sugar, in the same bowl, on purpose. Not decoration — brown
sugar carries more moisture than white, and that ratio is what decides whether a
cookie comes out chewy or crisp. Two numbers, one texture, before a single cookie
touches the oven.

**3. Brobots Cocoa Pudding Pods — thickened, not just heated**
3 tablespoons of cornstarch against 2¾ cups of milk. That ratio is the entire
difference between pudding and soup. Double the milk without doubling the cornstarch
and it never sets — the recipe *is* a locked equation wearing a dessert costume.

**4. Brobots Double-Choc Muffin Mechs — makes 12**
"Fill each cup ¾ full" isn't a style note. It's a batter-to-yield conversion — the
exact line that decides whether the same batch makes 12 regular muffins or 10 bigger
ones. Portion maths, hiding in one instruction nobody reads twice.
