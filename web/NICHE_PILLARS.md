# The Pillars

> Nine topics, one rule each — not just what to write about, but what kind of writing belongs there.
> A shape for this project's future writing, not a list of places to find it.

---

## What this is

This project's future writing is being split into nine different topics. Each one is a different everyday kind of maths — the number thinking hiding inside an ordinary moment, not a maths class. Each topic also sets its own tone and decides what kind of writing belongs inside it. That's the whole plan: nine rooms, each with its own rules, not one big pile of writing about everything.

Same tagline runs through every one of the nine rooms — **AI Wordplay! Explain the
Math!** — nine different flavors of the same move, each one still ending at the same
door: **GOPOD Yourself.**

## The nine topics

- **Food** — kitchen, recipes, portions, cost per serving.
- **Money** — budgeting and everyday practical figures.
- **Sport** — scores, stats and comparisons.
- **Fashion** — cost per wear, sizing, wardrobe decisions.
- **Language** — meaning, rhythm, wordplay, timing.
- **Personality** — people, character, comedy, what fits a room.
- **Survival** — resources, risk and reward, working through a scenario.
- **Access** — maths made reachable for anyone who was told they were bad at it.
- **Aftermath** — the write-up after the fact: what happened and what it meant.

## Why a topic is a rule, not just a subject

Naming a topic isn't just picking a label — it's a decision about what gets written and what gets turned away. Writing that starts in Food but drifts toward comparing numbers across a season belongs in Sport, or it doesn't get written at all under that topic. That decision, applied every time, is what stops nine topics from turning into one big pile that could have been about anything.

## How topics connect

The nine topics aren't sealed off from each other. Working out the cost of a meal is Food maths and Money maths at the same time — the same piece of writing can live in both, because the maths underneath is real either way. A sport comparison written with rhythm and wordplay borrows from Language while staying Sport at heart. Access isn't really a subject of its own — it's a way of writing that any of the other eight topics can use, aimed at someone who was told long ago that maths wasn't for them.

## Where this stands

The plan is written. Almost none of it is built yet. It's being started one topic at a time, on purpose — the first topic teaches the rest, and doing all nine at once was never the idea.

## The names make it findable

A made-up name is what makes any of this findable later, the same reason as everywhere else in this project — see [FUNNY_NAMINGS.md](FUNNY_NAMINGS.md).
