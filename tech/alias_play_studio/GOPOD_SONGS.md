# GOPOD Songs

> Two brobots. Small sequences labelled as songs. One workshop where every line gets written, tested, and changed before anyone in a room ever sees it.

---

## What This Place Is

A song here is one scripted piece the two brobots perform — written and rehearsed like a script, then recorded. This page is the shared workshop behind every one of them, where each one gets written, rehearsed, and improved before it's finished. Right now there are five songs. Each one teaches the two brobots something different about being funny, warm, or useful.

These are recorded pieces made for upload, not live performances.

A song and a song-video aren't quite the same thing. The song is what the brobots
actually do — timed and scored ahead of time, played for real on the Jetson, brobots and
terminal only, going quiet at any spot left open for a reporter to speak later. The
song-video is that same song after it's been through the edit: reporter voiceover
dropped into those quiet spots, news-style framing laid over the top, and — for the
newest song — real footage cut in around it. The song is what the brobots do. The
song-video is what you actually watch. (Songs are themselves built from smaller proven
pieces, arranged in order; the fuller breakdown of those lives in the operator's own
alias docs, not this page.)

Same pull runs under the songs below, plain version: a short, free taste first (Awaken —
the appetizer, no commitment). Then the real story (Interview — the sit-down worth
watching all the way through). Then the shareable finish (Bingo — built to get passed
around). Same move, three times, each one its own size. "AI Wordplay! Explain the Math!"
runs through every one of them, and every one ends the same door: **GOPOD Yourself.**

## Awaken

A brobot wakes up, checks that its own arms and head still move, tells you the weather, and says it is ready. There is no waiting around — one step follows the next until the brobot is set to go. It ends with the same line every time: "Ready to party! Did someone say GOPOD Yourself?"

What's still coming: nothing. This one is finished.

## Bingo

Bingo is actually two things wearing one name. The scored song — what this page
describes — is a full little sequence with a beginning, a middle, and an end: the two
brobots draw numbers together and give each other a hard time about it the whole way
through, timed and written ahead of time for the upsell video. Separately, there's a
real, live Bingo game the brobots can run for an actual room — different code, different
purpose, genuinely live rather than scored, not this page's subject.

What's still coming: a few spots in the song left open on purpose — jokes nobody's written yet, room for whoever does.

## Interview

Two brobots sit down together, and one interviews the other. There are seven questions: who are you, who built you, why does this work without the internet, and what does a normal day look like. This is the best writing in the whole set — sharp, funny, and specific. Behind the scenes it runs in two parts: first the brobots generate the actual answers (a short warm-up chat fills the wait), then they perform straight from what got generated — write, then play, never both at once.

What's still coming: a short opening description for this song — open for whoever wants to write it first.

## Is That You

Say a name, watch which brobot answers first — live, not scripted, on camera. Two
versions on the shelf: **Single**, the single-bot jewel (hold Doc's key alone, one
robot, the same real thing a single-Vector owner could run themselves), and **Multi**,
the two-brobot original (either robot's key, the real mix-up). Both bracket the exact
same live push-to-talk moment — the brobots' own awareness caught on camera,
leaked-footage style, decided live, never rehearsed. An earlier scripted four-line
stand-in for this same idea is archived now, made redundant once the real live version
existed to shoot instead.

What's still coming: both versions are wired and dry-verified — what's left is the take
itself, not more building.

## Baby Robots Sleep

A quiet, story-shaped piece — no back-and-forth, just one brobot telling where it came from. A small brobot tips over in a big, hard, electric world. Someone picks it up, holds it close, and rubs its back until it settles. That care is where the name "Doc" started.

What's still coming: it's on the normal song menu now — what's left is the video itself, its picture still needs filling in around the recorded audio. The music and words are done, the footage isn't.

## The Machine Behind the Songs

This one is the thing being built to play them. Every song above was
written by hand and, until now, needed its own separate machine to run it. That's slowly
changing: one shared machine, one growing songbook of scores, one control panel to pick a
song, choose where it starts and stops, and play it. Not five different machines anymore —
heading toward one.

What's still coming: this is a direction, not a finished thing. Right now that one shared
machine already plays three of the songs on this page, proven side-by-side against the old,
separate machines each of those used to need. It doesn't reach every song here yet, and the
everyday "play a song" button still points at the old machines for now, not the new one.
Both of those are still ahead.

---

## What Is Still Missing

There is one open question across every song: nobody has worked out how long a brobot should wait for a laugh. Timing a song for video is already solved — a pause lasts exactly as long as the edited version needs. Timing a live performance in a real room, with real people laughing at their own pace, is a different problem, and it has not been solved yet. That is the next thing to learn.
