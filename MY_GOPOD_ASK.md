## What's built

GOPOD is real. Two robots — the Brobots — that perform, explain, play. A whole layer that runs: the songs, the wordplay, the content engine, the voice. I built it. It works. You can see it move. That's the foundation, and it's mine.

Underneath that: a terminal-native studio that treats robot behavior like music — notes
into sequences into songs, a calibration bench, a playhead that scrubs through a
performance step by step. That's not the ask — it's what the person who takes the ask
gets to build on top of.

## Where the line is

I can do 100% of what I call the niche-buzz glamour:

- The photos and video (I can and must do that — lol)
- The initial public postings, the announcement
- The creative polish, the voice, the feel

That's my end. I've got it.

But there's a line, and above it is everything I can't carry alone:

- Getting multichat ready — the live push-to-talk cockpit, where you actually talk to GOPOD. The core loop already runs — push-to-talk, speech-to-text, an LLM, the robot talking back — proven and iterated over a dozen times. What's missing is turning that into one real, live, connected session. This is the core of what I need.
- Getting the websites ready — they're up, but "under construction." They need to become real.
- crushn8r.ca live — real email and newsletter subscriptions. This needs to actually work.
- Getting the social accounts ready — set up, running, on a schedule.
- Livestream and contest — for generating content, on a cadence.
- Social content generation and posting schedules — the machine that keeps it fed.
- The GOPOD layer itself — the whole thing above the glamour line. This is where I hit my ceiling.

### Start here, if you can only grab one thing

The list above is everything. But if you're the kind of person who wants one clear place to start — here it is: the live multichat cockpit. The GOPOD layer's beating heart.

Underneath it, the hard part's already built and running, in two separate proven pieces: the song-runner that pre-generates then plays clean, the event bus that proved decoupled control with Bingo, two Brobots that wake on one phrase and perform offline — and, separately, a real loop that already works today: push a key, capture speech, ask an LLM, get an answer back through the robot, iterated more than a dozen times. Neither piece is a sketch. What's missing is the wire between them — turning two proven engines into one real, live, connected session. That's the surface.

What "we're cooking" looks like in 60 days: a person can push a button, say something, and get Doc or Pip answering live — not a scripted song, a real exchange. If that clicks for you, everything else on the list gets easier, because the hardest layer just got a driver.

That's the one. Grab it, and you're not helping me grow the whole thing — you're building the part I can't reach, on top of the part I already made run.

Want something smaller first? The single-bot on-ramp — one Vector, LLM chat, no second robot — has two scoped, known fixes waiting: a hardcoded robot address that needs to move into config, and a broken spoken wake trigger. Either one is a real, contained first cut, not a warm-up exercise.
